## 1. Logline

> A wind-up lamplighter whose charging pillar has broken has exactly ninety-two turns of
> stored energy left, and one night to light the harbour beacon before the last ship home
> is wrecked on the reef. Every step costs a turn. The beacon needs a mainspring, not a
> match.

**Theme in one line:** *What you are made of is what you are for.*

## 2. The immediate hook (0:00 – 0:08)

Macro. A key turns in a brass back. *Click. Click. Click.*

On the fourth turn the ratchet **slips** — a spring tooth shears off and pings away into
the dark. The gauge needle, instead of climbing to full, sags and settles at **92**.

We do not know what we are looking at. We do not know where we are. Something irreversible
has already happened.

That is the entire first four seconds. The world is withheld until second 20.

**Why it works:** the hook opens mid-action on an unexplained mechanism, so the audience's
first question is *what is this*, not *where are we*. The failure lands at second four —
inside the window where viewers normally leave — and the number 92 lands at second eight,
becoming a promise the rest of the film keeps.

## 3. Protagonist

**WICK** — a wind-up brass lamplighter automaton, about 40 cm tall.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** | Light the Tidelight beacon before dawn so the returning ship survives the reef. |
| **Need** | To learn that her value was never the turns she had left, but what she spent them on. |
| **Flaw** | Methodical to a fault. She tries to solve a problem of sacrifice with arithmetic. |
| **Change** | From conserving herself to spending herself completely — and being met, at the end, by someone who spends on *her*. |

**Design.** Compact rounded body on four jointed limbs, one long lamp-pole. Aged brass,
verdigris, barnacle crust. Two shutter-lens eyes. **No mouth.**

Her chest is a glass drum containing a visible coiled mainspring and a needle gauge that
reads turns remaining. That gauge is the film's spine.

**She has no face to animate.** Emotion is carried entirely by shutter aperture, head tilt,
limb posture and gait rhythm. Her walk is a rhythm; as the spring weakens the rhythm
stutters, and the audience hears her dying before they see it.

### 3.1 The only two other characters

| Character | Role | Screen time | Treatment |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **THE RETURNING SHIP** | The stake — never a character we meet | 14 s | A low, broad silhouette with one raked mast and a single amber running light. Always distant, always behind rain and fog. Never close enough to need detail. |
| **THE CHILD** | The closing gesture | 17 s | Small, oversized coat, bare feet. Backlit and rim-lit, frequently in silhouette against dawn. The face is **never** resolved into readable features. |

Both exist to keep the film's cost down without shrinking its emotional range: the ship is
what she is spending herself on, and the child is what spends on her. The child is the only
human in the film, and appears only in SC10 — which means there is no human modelling, no
human facial animation and no lip sync anywhere in the production.

## 3A. Locations

Three sets. All hard-surface, all modular.

| Location | Scenes | Description | Build cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Winding Plaza** | SC01, SC02, SC03 | A barnacled stone quay at the bottom of a drowned harbour town. At its centre stands the winding pillar — a brass column with a key socket, now stripped and dead. Behind it the beacon tower rises out of the water. | Moderate: modular quay kit, one hero prop, one background tower. Lit at dusk only; the plaza is left behind at 01:32. |
| **The Flooded Streets** | SC04, SC05 | Half-submerged lanes of the old town, connected by a rusted gantry walkway. Still water — until the storm arrives here. | Moderate: repeating facade modules instanced along a curve, one hero gantry. |
| **The Tidelight Tower** | SC06, SC07, SC08, SC09, SC10 | A tapering iron lighthouse. Interior: a collapsed spiral stair. Exterior: riveted plates and handholds in gale-force wind. Top: the burner chamber, with a cold brass socket the exact diameter of a mainspring. | Highest build cost, but **carries five of ten scenes through relighting alone** — the best reuse ratio in the film. |

The tower is the load-bearing asset. Everything about SC06–SC10 is a relight, a camera
height, and a weather state on the same geometry.

## 4. Goal, obstacle, escalating conflict

**Goal:** light the beacon before the ship reaches the reef at dawn.

**Obstacle:** the winding pillar is dead — a stripped socket, a key that spins free. She
has 92 turns of stored energy and no way to make more. **Everything costs turns.**

The conflict escalates arithmetically. This is not a metaphor the film states; it is a
budget the audience can audit:

| Scene | What it costs | Cost | Gauge after |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| SC03 | Setting out across the plaza | 8 | 84 |
| SC04 | Climbing the flooded street | 13 | 71 |
| SC05 | Gantry crossing — the pole is lost, the spark is sheltered | 13 | 58 |
| SC06 | Forcing the sealed tower door | 18 | 40 |
| SC06 | The stair gives way | 9 | 31 |
| SC07 | Exposure on the rivets; the remaining stair collapses | 9 | 22 |
| SC07 | The outside climb in the gale | 16 | 6 |
| SC08 | The last pull to the burner | 5 | **1** |
| | **Total** | **91** | |

92 − 91 = **1**. She arrives at the burner with exactly one turn and nothing to spare.

The ignition costs one turn.

The ending gives one turn back.

**The film opens on four turns of a key that fail. It closes on one turn of a key that
holds.**

## 7. Visual storytelling

**Look.** Cinematic 3D, stylised-realistic "brass and barnacle." Real light behaviour,
exaggerated silhouette and colour. Not photoreal, not toon-shaded.

**Colour script.**

| Scenes | Key | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| SC01–03 | cold teal, one warm practical | isolation, dusk |
| SC04–05 | storm slate, deep teal, amber spark in the chest | threat, plus the thing worth protecting |
| SC06–07 | near-monochrome, high contrast, storm-white rim | the world reduced to effort |
| SC08 | black → full amber | the single largest contrast event in the film |
| SC09–10 | dawn rose-gold over wet stone | release, warmth returned |

**Lighting.** Almost entirely practical and motivated: one lamp, one spark, one beacon, one
dawn. Volumetric shafts through rain. Heavy silhouette work so shapes read at any size.

**Camera.** Anamorphic-flavoured widescreen framing, shallow depth of field on the hero,
long lenses for the tower's height. The camera stays **low** for the first two thirds to
hold her scale; it rises above her only after the ignition.

**Shot grammar.** Average shot ≈ 5.4 s (52–60 planned shots, inside the 1.5–8 s band).
Long static holds for emotional beats; short cuts only in SC05 and SC07. One deliberate
20-second hold in SC09.

**Art-direction rules.**

1. Every silhouette must read at 20 pixels tall.
2. One warm light source per scene, except the ignition.
3. The gauge is always legible when it changes; it is **never** cut away from during a decrement.
4. Nothing in frame is decorative — the set is drowned, so everything is repurposed.

**Signature image.** The amber spark glowing inside her glass chest while rain streaks the
glass. The whole second act in one frame.

## 8. Production complexity — deliberately engineered down

**Rating: MODERATE. Built to be CPU-renderable.**

| Constraint | Value |
|---|---|
| Characters to rig | **1** |
| Facial rigs | **0** |
| Locations to build | **3** |
| Scenes / shots | 10 / 52–60 |
| Total frames | 7 248 |
| Dialogue | none → no VO, no lip sync, no phonemes |

**Why this is achievable on CPU:**

- One hero character, hard-surface, with a two-shutter face. No blendshapes, no lip sync,
  no hair, no cloth.
- Zero dialogue removes voice recording, lip sync and phoneme work entirely.
- The only human appears for 17 seconds, backlit, never in readable close-up — near-zero
  human modelling cost.
- Three locations; the tower carries **five of ten scenes** through relighting alone.
- The ship is a silhouette asset behind rain and fog, never detailed.
- All VFX are particle or emission based. **No fluid, cloth, hair or fur simulation.**
- Modular, instanced architecture rather than unique geometry per building.
- A stylised-realistic look tolerates low sample counts plus denoising — the single biggest
  CPU render-time lever.

**Deliberately avoided:** crowds, dialogue and lip sync, fur/hair/cloth, fluid simulation
(water is a shaded plane with an animated normal map, largely hidden by rain and fog),
human faces in close-up, daytime exteriors with full GI, unique background geometry.

**Highest-risk elements and their mitigations:**

| Element | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| SC07 outside climb, 42 s | most animation on the hero rig | repeated grab-and-swing cycle with variation, not bespoke motion per shot |
| Rain across SC05–08 | particle volume and shading cost | camera-space rain plane plus fog, not world-space particles |
| SC08 ignition | high dynamic range bloom on a CPU renderer | composite the glow as an emission pass, not a volumetric solve |
| The gauge | small detail lost at 1080p | high-contrast dial, thick needle, framing rules guarantee screen area on every change |

**Render strategy.** Scene-level renders only (`13_RENDER/RENDER_MANIFEST.json`). Never a
single 7 248-frame job. Frames written individually so any shot can be re-rendered alone.

## 9. Retention strategy

**Hook deadline in `MASTER_CONFIG.json`: 8 s. Hook actual: 4 s.**

**The countdown device.** The chest gauge is diegetic and always readable when it changes.
A visible, arithmetically honest countdown converts every action into stakes without a line
of dialogue. The audience does the maths *with* the film — the cheapest possible form of
engagement, and the one that survives translation into every language.

**Turn cadence** (seconds): 4 · 32 · 65 · 92 · 122 · 158 · 186 · 205 · 228 · 262 · 282.
Maximum gap **36 s**, inside the 40-second rule.

**Silent viewing.** Fully comprehensible with the sound off. Colour, silhouette, framing and
the gauge carry 100 % of the plot. Music and mechanism sound deepen it but are never
load-bearing.

**No dead air.** No shot holds without a change in frame for more than 6 s — except the
deliberate 20-second hold in SC09, which is motivated by emotional decompression and
carries moving water and light.

**Loop design.** The closing macro of the key turning mirrors the opening macro frame for
frame, so the film loops cleanly and a rewatch begins in the right place.

**Rewatch reward.** On a second viewing the audience knows the socket reveal is coming, and
can watch SC01–SC07 as a machine being led to its purpose.

**Thumbnail frame.** SC08 — the amber ignition flooding the frame over a black silhouette.
Maximum contrast, immediate question.

**Targets.** Hook landed by 00:04 · premise, ship and deadline all established by 01:05 ·
mid-film guards at 02:55 (red arc) and 03:30 (gait breakdown) · **≥ 70 % average percentage
viewed.**

## 10. Originality attestation

All characters, designs, locations, story structure, beats and sound design in this brief
are **original** and were created for this project. No character, story, dialogue, scene,
distinctive design or trademarked element has been taken from — or deliberately imitated
from — any existing film, series, game, book or studio.

**Generic elements acknowledged.** Lighthouses, wind-up mechanisms, storms and sacrifices
are public-domain storytelling furniture. They are used here in an original combination with
an original protagonist, an original world, and an original central mechanic: a **hard
arithmetic countdown of turns spent**, auditable by the audience and closed by a twist that
is mechanical rather than magical.

**Music and audio.** Score to be composed original for this project. No library cues without
a recorded licence and provenance.

**Asset provenance.** Every asset will carry a provenance field in
`06_ASSETS/ASSET_INVENTORY.json` — `generated`, `authored`, or `licence + source`.
